# City 1999: Homeless in a Big City

> Status: idea (2026-11-22); trash loop built (2026-11-24, see "Trash" below); gameplay talked through (2026-11-25, see "Gameplay")

A possible game on top of NavKit: you are a homeless person in a big city around 1999.
You don't build a colony; you survive inside one that other people already run.
This doc lists what we already have, what is missing, and the smallest steps that give
the most. It follows [autarky.md](autarky.md): every item comes from somewhere and goes
somewhere, and nothing appears from nowhere.

---

## The core shift: from free to owned

In the forest everything is free: cut a tree, pick a berry. In a city **everything belongs
to someone**. A bench, a doorway, a can in a bin, a bed in a shelter. That tension (what
may I take, where may I sleep, who sends me away) *is* the game. Ownership is the one
genuinely new axis; most other parts reuse systems we have.

## Gameplay: from one bum to a can empire (talked through 2026-11-25)

One person in a city that runs by itself. You steer him with orders and marks; his own
autonomy fills everything in between.

### Three layers of steering

| Layer | What the player does | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | "search that bin", "sit there", "eat this" | built: the action queue, E / Shift+E (`action_queue.c`) |
| **Habits** | marks and routines: "my spot", "good bin", "stash here", "avoid this alley", "do the bin round in the morning" | not yet: designations reused |
| **Self** | nothing, he does it himself: needs, talks, scrounging, lingering, trash follow-ups | built (needs, social, queue interrupts) |

The middle layer makes it a game and not a puppet. In a colony a designation says "dig this
out"; for one person it says **what I know of the city and what I prefer**. His autonomy
uses your marks: hungry, he goes to the bins you marked good; tired, to the spot you claimed.
This ties in with "knowledge per mover" below: he knows the places he has seen, you write on top.

A **routine** joins the two: a saved queue tied to places and a time ("the round": bins A,
B, C, then the bottle machine) that he runs by himself whenever he's free. The `bottlerun`
scenario is one routine without the repeat. Building one: queue it with Shift+E, then
"make this a routine".

### Who wins

Critical need > your order now > routine > free time. Mood and traits can bend this, and
that is content, not a bug: a proud man hesitates to eat from a bin, a desperate one drops
your order for the can, a tired one says "not now" (Sims free will). How often he may say
no is open.

### Free time with meaning

Today an idle mover with no job or order handles needs, talks, scrounges when hungry
(Scavenger), and otherwise wanders (endless mode) or stands. In a colony jobs fill the day;
here **free time is the game**. When nothing presses he picks something himself, scored by
what he's a bit low on, who he is and where he is:

| Low on | He goes to | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| money | pick up cans on the way, check a bin, beg at a busy spot | foot traffic (`groundwear.c`) |
| comfort | sit at *his* spot, warm up at a grate | marks, heat stations |
| company | chat with another homeless person, sit near people | social (`talk.c`, `relations.c`) |
| boredom | look around, walk a street he doesn't know yet | knowledge per mover (later) |
| safety | stash his things, get away from police | stash mark, ownership (later) |

Each activity is a short action queue. This is the same work as moving the old `FREETIME_*`
states onto the queue (`../todo/architecture/action-queue-and-need-index.md`).

### Dead time with one agent

A colony fills time with ten people; one person mostly walks. Ways out: fast-forward while
he runs a routine, back to normal when something happens (cold, police, someone talks to
him); plan the day and watch; and a world that moves by itself (bins fill because NPCs eat,
shops open and close, the cold comes), so you plan around the world, not a timer.

### A day

- **Morning**: the bin round, cash in at the bottle machine, buy food at the stand.
- **Afternoon**: free time: talk, scrounge, look for a better spot.
- **Evening**: reach a warm or sheltered spot before the cold.
- **Night**: sleep; your things can be stolen, so where you sleep matters.

Pressure: cold, money, ownership (what may I take, where may I sleep).

### The arc

1. **Survive**: one person, orders + marks, his free time fills the gaps.
2. **Earn**: coins, the bottle machine, a shopping cart, a fixed round.
3. **Camp**: befriend other homeless people (relations exist); someone you trust joins:
   a shared spot, a shared stash.
4. **Can empire**: the colony tools come back. Camp members get rounds (jobs), stockpiles
   become the camp stash, carts haul cans, the city's bins are territory, rival collectors
   want the same bins.

The game grows into the colony system NavKit already has: you start steering one person
and end up running a small colony, with the same code.

### First playable slice (one evening)

1. Coins + bottle return machine (step 5 below), so the round pays.
2. Marks: "my spot", "good bin", "stash"; autonomy prefers them.
3. Routine: a saved, repeating queue.
4. A small block scenario with a cold night.

## Inventory

**Today** a mover holds three things: `carryingItem` (only during a job), `equippedTool`,
`equippedClothing`. Containers already work fully, including containers in containers.

**Proposal (Cataclysm-style): inventory is the containers on you or next to you.** No
separate inventory list on the mover. "What do I have?" = hands + worn containers +
a container I'm pushing / standing next to.

| What you have | Room | In our code |
|---|---|---|
| Just hands | 1 thing | `carryingItem` (exists) |
| A coat | Pockets: a few small things | `equippedClothing` (exists), clothing that is also a container |
| A bag / backpack | More | A worn container: the new `equippedContainer` slot |
| A shopping cart | A lot, but not *on* you | A container on the ground that you push |

What comes for free:
- Containers in containers, save/load, describe lines, spilling contents on death or drop.
- Other movers leave worn contents alone: `IsItemAccessible()` already refuses items whose
  container is carried or reserved.

### Built (2026-11-24, save v106): two hands, back, pockets

Three ways to have something, decided with the user:

| How | Examples | Hands free? | Who can reach in? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worn | pocket jacket (body slot = `equippedClothing`), backpack (`Mover.wornBack`) | yes | only the wearer |
| Held | plastic bag, a can, a log | no | only the holder |
| Pushed (later) | cart, wheelbarrow | no, both hands | anyone next to it |

- **Two hands** (`Mover.hands[2]`); `IF_TWO_HANDED` items (log, stripped log, raw stone,
  chest, bed, chair, carcass, trash bin) take both. Pushing will take both too.
- **Pockets only on a dedicated piece**: the Pocket Jacket (clothing + container, 3 stacks).
  Other clothing has none. Backpack: 10 stacks, worn on the back (`IF_WORN_BACK`). Plastic
  Bag: 4 stacks, held in one hand. (Stack counts for now; volume per item type is next.)
- `src/entities/inventory.c`: `MoverHold`, `MoverWear`, `MoverDropHand/Back/All`, and
  `MoverStow` = pockets, then backpack, then a bag in a hand, then a free hand. Worn/held items
  are `ITEM_CARRIED` reserved by the mover (like the tool); what is inside them is
  `ITEM_IN_CONTAINER`, out of reach for others (`IsItemAccessible`). Dropped containers keep
  their contents; death drops everything. Tools stay as they are (not in the hands).
- Describe lines: "Hands: Plastic Bag (1 inside) / Soda", "Back: Backpack (2 inside)",
  pockets on the clothing line. Scenario `inventory` (six movers kitted out); tests
  `test_inventory`. State audit checks held/worn items.
- Found while doing it: the once-a-second "stale reservations" sweep in needs.c also released
  worn clothing and the tool; it now skips worn and held gear.
- Not yet: jobs and hands (a job carries as before, in its own field; a mover holding things
  should drop the bag for a two-handed haul and pick it up after), volume, the cart,
  searching bins into the inventory, drawing held items.

### Volume and weight (built 2026-11-24, save v108)

- **Volume**: litres per item type (`ItemVolume`: set for city things, others 1.5 x their
  kg) and per container (`ContainerDef.capacityL`: pockets 2, plastic bag 10, backpack 25,
  bin 12). A full bag takes up more room itself. Colony containers (basket, chest, pot) keep
  counting stacks (capacity 0) for now.
- **Weight**: carried weight per mover (`MoverCarriedWeight`, inventory.c): hands and
  pockets in full, **the back counts half**. Walking speed follows it (same curve as a job's
  load), and over 5 kg it tires like hauling. **Lifting limit** setting `carryLimit` (30 kg):
  past it nothing more is held or stowed.
- **Contents lists**: each container keeps its own list (`Item.firstChild / nextSibling`,
  `ContainerLink / ContainerUnlink`), so "what is in this bag" no longer scans every item.
  Rebuilt after every load; the state audit checks them.
- Describe: "Back: Backpack (0.4/25 L)", "pockets 1.7/2 L", "Load: 20.0 kg of 30 (walking 71%)".
- Step B done (below).

### Jobs and hands (built 2026-11-24, no save change)

- A job's load sits in the hands: `MoverFreeHands` counts it (a log: both), describe shows
  "Hands: Oak Log, hauling (both)". Its weight counts in the same total (`MoverCarriedWeight`
  adds the job's load live), so the separate job-weight code in walking speed is gone.
- Before a job picks something up (7 places in jobs.c + fuel), `MoverMakeHandsFor` puts down
  what is in the way (a bag, with its contents) at the mover's feet, still reserved for it.
  Berries fit beside a bag: nothing goes down.
- When no job comes for a while (0.15 h idle), the mover goes back for it
  (`FREETIME_FETCHING_OWN`) and holds it again; while there is work it keeps working. Can't
  get there or it's gone: forgotten, free for anyone. Death frees it. The set-aside list is
  saved (`Mover.setAside`, save v109).
- Fixed with it: a load clears every item reservation and only gave jobs theirs back, so worn
  clothing, the tool, backpack, held and set-aside things lost theirs on every load (an old
  problem for clothing and tools). `ReserveAllMoverGear()` gives them back after a load.
- One "its own gear" check (`ItemIsMoverGear`: tool, clothing, back, hands, set aside) for
  the stale-reservation sweep and the state audit.
- Plastic bags no longer stack (two bags merging in a stockpile dropped one's contents).
- Scenario `hands-jobs` (two haulers with a bag + a can, logs and berries to haul). Colony
  games run identically (survival, island, crafting, belt-workshop, dollhouse compared).

### The shopping cart

- **Pushing**: it moves with you; you're slower and can't take stairs or ladders.
- **Let go** and it is just a container on the ground: you reach in from an adjacent cell,
  and **so can anyone else**. Sleep away from it and it may be emptied or taken: ownership
  happens by itself.
- **It isn't yours**: it belongs to the supermarket, so taking it is theft. 1999 carts have
  a **coin lock**: a coin frees one. Ties coins, keys and ownership together.
- A cart with cardboard in it can be a level-1 sleeping spot, like sleeping in a vehicle in Cataclysm.
- The cart is the same mechanic as a colony sledge or wheelbarrow (a container you hold on to);
  see "Pushables" in [../research/vehicles.md](../research/vehicles.md).

### What to add

1. **Volume instead of count.** Containers now hold "max N distinct stacks" (`maxContents`).
   Pockets must take a can but not a blanket: one volume number per item type, checked in
   `CanPutItemInContainer()`. Weight (speed, tiredness) can come later.
2. **Clothing that is a container**: a container def for coat types.
3. **Pushing**: a mover state "holding object X", X moves along and slows the mover. Pathing
   must avoid ladders/stairs for that mover: the same per-mover question as keys.
4. **Reach**: hands + worn + containers in adjacent cells.

### Cost with many movers

- The slot is one int per mover: a mover without a bag costs nothing.
- **Items are the real limit**: `MAX_ITEMS` is 25,000 in total, and many systems loop over
  the whole item array. 10,000 NPCs with a bag and three things each = 40,000 items.
- So: real bags for the player and a few other homeless people. Ordinary passers-by have
  at most "the can in their hand", which becomes a real item only when they throw it away.
- Raising the item cap is its own topic: see "Item cap" below.

## Trash, bins and deposit bottles

Bins do **not** refill on a timer. They fill because other people in the city ate and
drank. The trash is a trace of the city's life: busy places get full bins, quiet places
stay empty.

### Built (2026-11-24, save v104)

- Items: Soda (drink) leaves an Empty Can, Snack (food) leaves a Wrapper
  (`ItemLeavesBehind()` in `items.c`); Trash Bin, a container (4 stacks, trash stacks of 5:
  ~20 pieces) with `IF_FIXED`, so no haul job carries it off. Sandwiches etc. leave nothing,
  so the colony games don't change.
- The can in hand is **virtual**: `Mover.heldTrash` (a type), a real item only in the bin or
  on the ground. Costs nothing per NPC.
- After eating/drinking (`needs.c`, `GetRidOfTrash`): nearest bin with room
  (`ContainerHasRoomFor`) within setting `trashBinWalk` cells (12) → `FREETIME_SEEKING_BIN`;
  none: litter at the mover's feet. A bin that filled up on the way: dropped next to it.
  Traits **Tidy** (twice as far) and **Slob** (only a bin within 2 cells).
- Scenario `trash`: snack stand, bins at 4/14/32 cells, 10 wandering movers. Measured:
  3000 ticks A 18 + 2 litter (the slob); 10k A full, B 12, litter 12; 30k A and B full,
  litter 41; with `trashBinWalk 30` bin C fills too. Tests: `test_hunger` "trash".
- Placeholder sprites (no can/bin sprites in the atlas yet). Litter in a colony with a
  stockpile gets hauled like any item (stockpiles allow all types by default).
- Not yet: the sink (garbage truck / sweeper), deposit bottles, scavenging bins, litter as
  area quality, bins built by the player.

### The loop

1. **Consuming leaves something behind.** A soda leaves an *empty can*, a sandwich a
   *wrapper*, a beer an *empty bottle* (with deposit). One "leaves behind" field per item
   type (none exists today: eating just removes the item).
2. **The NPC gets rid of it.** Same tier pattern as bladder relief in `needs.c`
   (toilet → bush → floor): nearest bin with room → ground (litter). A trait decides who
   walks further to a bin and who drops it on the spot.
3. **The bin is a container.** Putting trash in uses `PutItemInContainer()`; full is full.
4. **The sink:** a garbage truck / street sweeper on a schedule empties bins and picks up
   litter. Gives timing: be at the bins before the truck comes.

### Scrounging (built 2026-11-24, save v107)

Searching bins for food, driven by hunger. The food has to come from somewhere, so:

- **Leftovers from big portions.** A Burger is a big portion (nutrition 1.0). An eater who
  needs less leaves the rest as Leftovers (units of 0.1, spoil within 12 h):
  `ItemLeftoverUnits()`. Physical, no dice: the starving finish it, the merely hungry don't.
  The diner throws them in the bin with the wrapper (held in a hand on the way; no bin:
  on the ground). Later a second source: shops throwing out spoiled stock.
- **Scavenger** trait (not rolled at random: a game/scenario sets it). It keeps its own
  leftovers (pockets/bag), and **for now also has "no money"**: it takes no food from
  stockpiles. That stand-in goes when coins and ownership exist.
- **Hunger chain** (`StartFoodSearch`): food the mover carries first (`EatFromInventory`),
  then stockpiles (not for scavengers), food on the ground, berries, then **a trash bin**
  (scavengers at once, anyone else only when starving): the nearest reachable bin with
  something edible in it, by walking (`StartScrounging`), one searcher per bin (the bin is
  reserved), rummage 0.2 h, eat the best find; a scavenger stows the rest.
- Scenario `scrounge` (the trash street selling burgers, 10 diners, 2 scavengers): 30k ticks,
  13 meals each, the bins emptied of leftovers. They also eat leftovers lying next to full
  bins. Tests: `test_hunger` "scrounging".
- Found on the way: the trash scenario's stand spawned its stock on the ground (not in the
  stockpile), so restocks piled up; fixed (`StandRestock`), its numbers changed a little.
- Next: collecting cans (needs coins + the bottle machine), a mood effect for eating from a
  bin (shame vs. Hardy/Stoic), shops throwing out stale food.

### What falls out of it

- **Bins fill where people walk**: station, park bench, snack bar. Same signal as
  `groundwear.c` foot traffic.
- **Deposit bottles are choices for everyone.** A thrifty NPC returns their own bottle;
  a lazy one tosses it (your income); a kind one sets it *next to* the bin, like people do
  for bottle collectors.
- **Competition**: other collectors search the same bins.
- **Litter is a signal**: lots of it means no bins nearby or a run-down street. Can feed
  room/area quality and mood.
- Still autarkic: the can came from a shop that got it delivered, and goes to the truck.

## Money and exchange

- **Coin item** (one item type, stacks).
- **Exchange stations**: input → coins or coins → goods. They are stations with a service
  queue:
  - *Bottle return machine* (supermarket): deposit bottles → coins.
  - *Shop counter / snack stand*: coins → food, drink (the source of the cans above).
  - *Soup kitchen*: free food at set hours, long queue.
- **Begging / busking**: stand on a spot; passers-by give a coin with a chance that grows
  with foot traffic and the giver's mood/traits. Busking could reuse the
  [ensemble stations](../todo/ensemble-stations.md) idea: playing music at a seat, coins
  scale with traffic.
- Where do NPCs' coins come from? Keep it simple at first: city NPCs have an allowance.
  A full wage/economy loop is later.

### Thoughts so far (2026-11-25, parked: the user wants to think before tackling money)

- **Item or number?** Coins as items make dropping, finding, theft and a busker's hat free,
  but a purse per mover eats the item cap (25,000) with thousands of NPCs. Leaning to a
  **hybrid**: money is a number on whoever holds it (a mover's purse, a till, the bottle
  machine) and only becomes a coin item when it lies in the world (dropped, lost, in a hat,
  in a cart); picking it up makes it a number again.
- **The loop**: outside the city (commuters, allowance, later wages) -> NPC purses -> snack
  stand's till -> a can in the bin -> you -> bottle machine, which pays from the shop's
  till (a shop that sells nothing can't pay deposits) -> your purse -> food.
- **Catch**: NPCs eat snacks for free today, and that fills the bins. Once paying is
  required they need money or the can loop stops: NPC purses belong in the first step.
- **Buying**: for-sale food is skipped by the food search unless you can pay; buying is a
  queue (walk to the till, wait in line via `service.c`, pay, take). `FURNITURE_TILL` /
  `CAP_SELLING` exist. First version unstaffed; shopkeepers come with Town's jobs and hours.
- **Plan (user liked it, 2026-11-25; not started)**. Lever: a snack stand already is a
  stockpile, so a stockpile gets a price; price 0 = free, so every game stays as it is until
  a stand has a price.
  1. *Money itself, no behaviour change*: `Mover.cents` (whole cents, saved, a describe line
     "ƒ 3,25"); `ITEM_COIN` for money on the ground (stack count = amount, picking it up
     adds to the purse); dev command `money <amount>`. Tests.
  2. *A stand with a price*: `Stockpile.price` and `Stockpile.cash` (the till); the food
     search skips a priced stockpile you can't pay; taking a unit pays at once (no queue or
     till walk yet). Prices as a column in the item defs (snack 1,50, soda 1,25, can deposit
     0,25). NPCs get starting money (setting `startMoney`) so `trash` still fills its bins
     the same.
  3. *The bottle machine*: furniture plus an `ACT_CASH_IN` queue step (cans from the bag,
     money from the shop's till into the purse); `bottlerun` becomes the whole loop:
     collect, cash in, buy a snack.
  4. *Tidy*: the Scavenger's "no money = no stockpile food" becomes "can't pay".
  Later, each on its own: a queue at the till (new service kind), shopkeepers, money falling
  out (dropped, robbed), begging, income from outside over time, Town on the same money.
  One save version bump (step 1 or 2).
- **Open**: hybrid or full coin items; guilders and cents (1999) or neutral coins; does
  Town use the same money?
- Meanwhile milestone 1 (a cold night) needs no money: marks, free time with meaning,
  sleeping spots with levels, a small block.

## Ownership and access

- An `owner` field on items, furniture and zones (a mover, a "public" value, or a
  business/household id).
- Item search in jobs and needs skips owned items unless the mover is willing to steal
  (trait, desperation). Taking it is theft: witnesses, reputation, police.
- **Zones** (like stockpiles: a rectangle with rules): owner, opening hours, who may
  enter. A shop at night, a station hall after the last train, a private porch.
- **Doors** that let only some movers through. Pathfinding needs a per-mover or per-group
  passability check here; check the cost in the HPA* graph before committing to it.
- Pays off in the colony games too: "my bed", "my stash", theft. The freetime doc lists
  furniture ownership as deferred.

### Keys and locks

Access as a physical thing instead of a rule: a lock on a door, a key as an item.

- **Lock**: a door (`CELL_DOOR` exists, walkable for everyone today) gets a lock id, or none.
- **Key**: an item with the lock id it opens. It lives in the mover's inventory
  (`equippedContainer`), so it can be lost, dropped, found, stolen, given.
- **Who has a key** follows ownership: the shop owner has the shop key, a shelter gives
  you a locker key for one night.
- Everything else falls out of keys being items:
  - a *key-cutting kiosk* is a station (key + coins → copy),
  - *lockpicking* is a tool with a quality, takes time, can be seen,
  - *breaking a door* is salvage: noise, witnesses, a broken door stays open,
  - *squatting*: change the lock on an empty building and it's yours (ownership by key),
  - colony games: lock the food store so nobody eats the winter stock.

**Pathfinding cost, cheapest first:**
1. Plan as today (doors walkable for all). A mover without the key who reaches a locked
   door "tries the handle", remembers that door as closed *for itself*, and replans with
   that cell avoided. Needs only a per-search avoid list, no graph changes. Also looks
   realistic: people try doors.
2. Only if (1) wastes too many trips: a lock-group bitmask on HPA* abstract edges and a
   key bitmask per mover, checked during the search. Reachability regions would then be
   a superset (a region behind a locked door still counts as reachable), so "can I get
   there?" checks must handle a failed search.

## Stations in the city

The capability idea ([capability-based-tasks.md](../todo/architecture/capability-based-tasks.md))
fits the city without new systems, only new flags on `capabilityGrid`:

| Thing | Capability |
|---|---|
| Heating grate, warm air vent | heat source (and it really warms cells via `temperature.c`) |
| Bridge, porch, bus shelter | shelter (roof, no walls) |
| Cardboard, bench, sleeping bag, shelter bed | sleeping, levels 1-3 |
| Bin | trash in / scavenge |
| Public tap, fountain | water source (already a flag) |
| Public toilet | bladder (already furniture) |

Why stations suit this game: what you can do depends on what is standing around you, not
on which building you placed. The same "warm up" works at a grate, a burning barrel or a
shelter radiator; the same "sleep" at cardboard (1), a bench (2) or a shelter bed (3).

What's built is only the kitchen version (an on/off bitmask, two flags, a square radius
around a workshop). To make it work here we need, in short:

- **Levels** per capability (cardboard 1 vs shelter bed 3).
- **The world as provider**: bridges and porches give shelter (no sky above), grates give heat.
- **Needs use stations** (cold → nearest heat), not only recipes.
- **Reachable, not just near**: the shop's oven behind a locked door is not yours.
- **Access filter**: owner, keys, opening hours.
- **Direct action** for the player character, next to bills (colony) and needs.
- **Exchange + queue** at a station (bottle machine, snack stand).

Full list, order and the smallest proof (heat): see "Where we are (2026-11)" in
[capability-based-tasks.md](../todo/architecture/capability-based-tasks.md).

## Salvage: the city's tree-felling

"Everything comes from the world" still holds: the city is a big deposit of parts.
Taking things apart gives materials: a pallet becomes planks, a shopping cart metal,
cardboard boxes become a shelter. Salvage recipes are the city's version of cutting
trees, and they feed the same building system (a cardboard hut is a blueprint).

## Time and schedules

The city needs time more than the village, but mostly for **places and flows**, not hard
blocks per mover. One small primitive serves all layers: a time window
`{startHour, endHour, days}`.

| Layer | What | Village | City |
|---|---|---|---|
| World clock | exists | yes | yes |
| Soft rhythm per mover | personal offset on need thresholds (tired sooner at night); hook for night owl / early bird traits | yes (schedule step 1) | yes |
| Commitments | "be at X during W" for movers with a workplace (shopkeeper 9-17, garbage truck 6:00) | with occupations | yes |
| Opening hours | time window on a station/zone; resolvers skip closed places | maybe | yes |
| Spawners | arrival/departure curve per entry point (commuters by train at 8, leave at 18, despawn) | no | yes, makes rush hour |

Milestone 1 (cold night) needs no schedule: night as world state (cold, dark) exists and
the player sleeps from the energy need. The first real need is milestone 2 (shops, garbage truck).

## Item cap

`MAX_ITEMS` is 25,000: one static array of `Item` (~60 bytes each, ~1.5 MB). Raising the
number itself is trivial (100,000 = ~6 MB; save files only write up to the high-water
mark). The cost is everything that loops over the **whole** array. As of 2026-11-22 there
are ~57 such loops (`for (int i = 0; i < itemHighWaterMark; i++)`):

| Kind | Where | How often | Problem at 100k+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every tick | `SpoilageTick`, `TemperatureTick`, `ItemsTick`, `BuildItemSpatialGrid` (items.c) | 4x per tick | Grows with all items, even ones that don't spoil or aren't hot |
| Per mover looking for work/food | WorkGivers in jobs.c (craft, clothing, water pot, crops...), `needs.c` food/drink search | Per mover, per attempt | movers x items: the real killer with many movers |
| Per container operation | `MoveContainer`, `SpillContainerContents`, `CanPutItemInContainer`, `ForEachContainedItem` (containers.c) | Every put/take/drop | No child list: each op scans everything. Bags on many movers make this hot |
| Per event | `DropItemsInCell`, `PushItemsOutOfCell`, stockpile edits | Per dig/build/edit | Fine now, slow with many items |
| Spawning | `SpawnItem` searches for the first free slot from 0 | Per spawn | O(items) per spawn |

### Steps, cheapest first

1. **Free list** for `SpawnItem` (O(1) spawn).
2. **Container child list** (`firstChild` / `nextSibling` on Item): container ops become
   O(contents). Needed for inventory anyway.
3. **Small lists for the per-tick loops**: only items that spoil, only hot items. The
   tick then costs what is changing, not what exists.
4. **Searches through indexes**: per-type lists or the item spatial grid for the WorkGiver
   and needs scans (large-worlds.md already did this for some needs scans).
5. **Then raise `MAX_ITEMS`** and measure with a stress scenario (many movers, many items),
   `--profile`. Check the tests that use `MAX_ITEMS` (18 places) still run fast.

Perf checks (same as the large-world work): `--profile` on 3000-tick runs (the first ~600
ticks after a load are a path-planning burst, don't judge on those), plus `make test` and
`make scenarios-check`. Only optimize against a target from real play, not a stress test
for its own sake (agreed 2026-11-22).

### Fewer items instead of more

- **Stacks**: `stackCount` + `stacking.c` exist. 20 cans in a bag can be one item with
  `stackCount` 20.
- **Virtual items** for passers-by: "the can in my hand" is a type on the mover, a real
  item only when thrown away.
- Later: items in unloaded chunks (chunked-world-plan step 7).
